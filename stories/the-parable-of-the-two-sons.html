Parables & Metaphors

The Parable of the Two Sons

Discover why a changed response and obedient action matter more than an unfulfilled promise.

Matthew 21:28–32

Introduction

Read this English paraphrase of the biblical account alongside the original companion booklet. Context notes, devotional applications, reflection questions, and the closing prayer are study aids rather than additional biblical text.

The Bible Story

A father asked one son to work in his vineyard. The son refused at first, but later changed his mind and went. The father asked his other son the same thing. That son agreed to go, but did not. Jesus asked which son had done the father’s will. His listeners answered that it was the first. Jesus then spoke about tax collectors and prostitutes responding to John’s message, while his hearers had not believed John or changed their minds even after seeing that response. This is a paraphrase of Matthew 21:28–32.

Historical and Biblical Context

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

This is the Two Sons in Matthew’s Gospel, distinct from the story often called the Prodigal Son. Jesus connects the sons’ different responses with believing John and changing one’s mind. The passage contrasts spoken agreement with actual response.

Spiritual Lessons

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

The booklet emphasizes actions that express obedience, repentance that brings change, and God’s welcome of those who turn toward him. Related devotional readings include James 2:17, Ezekiel 18:21, and Luke 15:7. These passages are study connections rather than part of the parable.

Practical Life Applications

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Identify a promise you have made but not acted on. Choose a realistic next step and follow through. If you have resisted doing what is right, acknowledge it and change course instead of allowing an earlier refusal to define your response.

Reflection Questions

  1. What is the difference between the two sons’ words and actions?
  2. Where do your actions need to catch up with what you say you believe?
  3. What change of direction could you put into practice today?

Closing Prayer

Lord God, help our actions reflect our faith. Give us humility to change our minds when we are wrong and courage to follow through in obedience. Amen.
