Parables & Metaphors

The Parable of the Good Samaritan

Discover how compassion becomes action through the Samaritan who stopped to care for a wounded stranger.

Luke 10:30–37

Introduction

What does it mean to love our neighbour? Jesus’ parable invites us to respond to suffering with mercy. This page adapts the English material in your companion booklet. The story is a paraphrase; reflection questions and prayer are devotional additions.

The Bible Story

A man travelling from Jerusalem to Jericho was attacked by robbers. They took his clothing, beat him, and left him half dead. A priest saw him and passed by on the other side. A Levite also saw him and passed by. A Samaritan came along and had compassion. He approached the injured man, treated and bandaged his wounds, placed him on his own animal, and brought him to an inn to care for him. The next day he gave the innkeeper two denarii and promised to repay any additional expense when he returned. Jesus asked which of the three had been a neighbour to the injured man. The answer was the one who showed mercy. Jesus called his listener to act in the same way. This is a paraphrase of Luke 10:30–37.

Historical and Biblical Context

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

The parable names a priest, a Levite, and a Samaritan. It does not tell us why the first two passed by, so we should not invent their motives. The focus of the closing exchange in Luke 10:36–37 is the mercy shown by the person who helped.

Spiritual Lessons

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

The booklet emphasizes compassion for all, practical help that may require sacrifice, and kindness across social and cultural barriers. The Samaritan’s compassion leads to concrete care. For further devotional study, the booklet connects these themes with Colossians 3:12, Galatians 6:2, and John 13:34. These are related teachings rather than part of the parable itself.

Practical Life Applications

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Notice a person whose needs you might otherwise overlook. Consider a practical act of help: listening, sharing a meal, offering appropriate support, or contributing time and resources. Examine whether differences in background are limiting your willingness to show kindness. These suggestions are devotional applications, not added events in the biblical account.

Reflection Questions

  1. What does the Samaritan’s response teach you about showing mercy?
  2. What barrier might keep you from caring for someone different from you?
  3. What practical act of compassion can you take this week?

Closing Prayer

Lord God, give us hearts that notice suffering and respond with mercy. Help us love our neighbours across differences and turn compassion into practical care. Guide our words, time, and resources toward kindness. Amen.
